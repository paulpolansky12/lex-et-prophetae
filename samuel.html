Notes

Dates are approximate. Solomon’s follow the Thiele chronology (970–931), and David’s forty years run back from there. Saul’s are the least certain: the Hebrew of 1 Samuel 13:1, which should give his age and the length of his reign, is damaged, and the forty years of Acts 13:21 is the usual figure.

The scenes follow the order of the books, which is mostly but not always the order of events. 2 Samuel 21–24 is an appendix gathered from different points in David’s reign.

Older Catholic Bibles (Douay-Rheims) call 1–2 Samuel “1–2 Kings,” 1 Kings “3 Kings,” and 1 Chronicles “1 Paralipomenon,” and spell many names differently, such as Heli for Eli or Bethsabee for Bathsheba. Those spellings are in each person’s details.

Chronicles starts its story at Saul’s death (1 Chr 10; chapters 1–9 are genealogies) and leaves out David’s outlaw years, Bathsheba and Uriah, and Absalom’s revolt. It adds the long preparations for the Temple in chapters 22–29.

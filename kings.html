Notes

Dates follow the widely used Thiele chronology. Scholars differ by a few years in places, and some kings ruled alongside their fathers for a time; those co-regencies are noted in each king’s details. Blocks start where the king ruled alone.

Very short reigns, like Zimri’s seven days, are stretched on the chart so they can be read and tapped. That nudges a few names a little lower than their true date.

Older Catholic Bibles (Douay-Rheims) call 1–2 Kings “3–4 Kings” and spell many names differently, such as Ochozias for Ahaziah or Ezechias for Hezekiah. Those spellings appear in each person’s details.

Kings and Chronicles tell the same history from two angles: Kings explains the exile and covers both kingdoms; Chronicles follows David’s line and the Temple, and often adds prophets and episodes Kings leaves out.

Who ruled when

The kings behind these books, oldest first. Persian dates are firm, fixed by Babylonian and Persian records.

  1. Shalmaneser V, Sennacherib, Esarhaddon Assyria, 727–669The kings of Tobit 1, under whom the northern tribes were carried to Nineveh.
  2. Nebuchadnezzar II Babylon, 605–562Took Jerusalem in 597 and burned it in 586. Judith borrows his name for a “king of the Assyrians.”
  3. Cyrus II Persia, 559–530Took Babylon in 539; the decree and the first return (Ezra 1–3).
  4. Cambyses II 530–522Conquered Egypt. Not named in the Bible; the building stood still.
  5. Darius I 522–486Haggai and Zechariah; the Temple finished in 515 (Ezra 5–6).
  6. Xerxes I, Ahasuerus 486–465Esther; the complaint of Ezra 4:6. Defeated by the Greeks at Salamis in 480.
  7. Artaxerxes I 465–424The letters of Ezra 4:7–23; Ezra in 458; Nehemiah in 445 and again about 432; Malachi.
  8. Darius II 423–404Named in Nehemiah 12:22. The Elephantine papyri of 407 mention Sanballat’s sons and the high priest Johanan (Neh 12:22–23).

The prophets

Three prophets belong to the return. Haggai and Zechariah got the Temple built; Malachi spoke to the community that had it and grew careless. The prophets of the exile itself, Jeremiah, Baruch, Ezekiel and Daniel, are on the divided kingdom page.

Notes

Dates are approximate for Tobit, Judith and the undated prophets. Persian reigns are firm. The traditional order puts Ezra (458) before Nehemiah (445); some scholars put Ezra later, in 398, under Artaxerxes II.

Esther exists in two forms. The Hebrew has ten chapters and never names God; the Greek adds six passages. Jerome moved them to the end of the book and numbered them there as 10:4–16:24; the RSV-2CE prints them back in their places but keeps his numbers, so the book opens at 11:2. The chapter picker lists them in RSV-2CE reading order, and each one’s chapter line gives where to find it in the Douay.

Tobit and Judith survive mainly in Greek. Jerome translated them quickly from Aramaic copies, and the Vulgate and Douay-Rheims text differs from modern translations in wording and verse numbers. This page follows the RSV-2CE.

Older Catholic Bibles call Ezra and Nehemiah 1 and 2 Esdras, and spell many names differently, such as Zorobabel for Zerubbabel or Mardochai for Mordecai. Those spellings are in each person’s details. Verse numbers follow the RSV-2CE. In Ezra, Nehemiah and the prophets the Douay-Rheims numbers almost always match; where they don’t, the Douay number is given.

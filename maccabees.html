The story in scenes

331 BC. Alexander takes the Persian empire. The return from Babylon under Persia is on the exile and return page; Daniel, whose visions describe the years told here, is on the divided kingdom page.

The two books are not volumes one and two. They overlap: 2 Maccabees 3–7 tells at length what led to the revolt, and 1 Maccabees 1 tells it in a chapter. From there the scenes follow 1 Maccabees and bring in 2 Maccabees wherever it covers the same ground.

Who ruled when

The kings behind these books, oldest first, then the high priests. The books date events by the Seleucid era, which counts from 312 BC; dates here may be a year out either way.

  1. Alexander the Great Macedon, 336–323Conquers the Persian empire; his generals divide it (1 Macc 1:1–9).
  2. The Ptolemies Egypt, 301–200Rule Judea for a century, lightly. Not told in these books.
  3. Antiochus III the Great Syria, 223–187Takes Judea from Egypt in 200. Beaten by Rome in 190 and made to pay, which is why his sons need money (1 Macc 8:6–8).
  4. Seleucus IV Philopator 187–175Sends Heliodorus for the Temple treasury (2 Macc 3). Murdered by him.
  5. Antiochus IV Epiphanes 175–164Sells the high priesthood, plunders the Temple in 169, forbids the Law in 167. Dies in Persia.
  6. Antiochus V Eupator 164–162A boy, with Lysias as regent. Beth-zechariah; the end of the persecution.
  7. Demetrius I Soter 162–150Sends Bacchides and Nicanor. Judas dies in his reign.
  8. Alexander Balas 150–145Makes Jonathan high priest in 152, while still a pretender.
  9. Demetrius II Nicator 145–138Confirms Jonathan, cheats him, and in 142 frees Simon from tribute. Captured by the Parthians.
  10. Antiochus VI and Tryphon 145–138A child king and the officer who crowned him, killed him and replaced him. Tryphon murders Jonathan.
  11. Antiochus VII Sidetes 138–129Confirms Simon, then demands the citadel back. The last strong Seleucid.

The high priests:

  1. Onias III to 175The last to hold the office by lawful succession.
  2. Jason 175–172His brother, by purchase.
  3. Menelaus 172–162By a higher bid; not of the high-priestly family.
  4. Alcimus 162–159Of Aaron’s line, appointed by Demetrius I.
  5. No high priest 159–152Seven years’ vacancy.
  6. Jonathan 152–143Appointed by Alexander Balas. The first Hasmonean high priest.
  7. Simon 143–134Confirmed by the people in 140, “till there should arise a faithful prophet.”

Notes

The two books were written separately. 1 Maccabees was composed in Hebrew about 100 BC and survives only in Greek; it covers 175 to 134 BC. 2 Maccabees was written in Greek, as an abridgment of a five-volume history by Jason of Cyrene, with two letters set in front of it; it covers about 180 to 161 BC. Neither is in the Hebrew Bible. Both are in the Greek and Latin Bibles and in the Catholic canon. There are a 3 and a 4 Maccabees, which are not.

Dates in the books are years of the Seleucid era, counted from 312 BC, so that “the hundred and forty-fifth year” is 167 BC. The two books do not always start the year in the same month, which is why modern dates for the same event can differ by a year.

Older Catholic Bibles call these books 1 and 2 Machabees and spell many names differently, such as Mathathias, Jonathas or Ptolemee. Those spellings are in each person’s details. Verse numbers follow the RSV-2CE. The Douay-Rheims runs up to three verses ahead in 1 Maccabees 1 and one verse ahead in parts of 1 Maccabees 12–13 and 2 Maccabees 2, 12 and 15; where a quotation falls in one of those places, the Douay number is given too.

Quotations follow the Douay-Rheims, with its name forms left as printed inside quotation marks. Where the Douay, which follows the Latin, parts from the Greek in a way that matters, the note says what the Greek has.

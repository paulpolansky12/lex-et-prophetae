Lex et Prophetae

A reader’s companion to the Old Testament: who’s who, what’s beneath the surface, and how it all connects.

Sections follow the story from beginning to end. They open as they’re finished, starting from the middle.

  1. The Law

    Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy

    Creation to the edge of the promised land, and the covenant everything after it is measured against.

    Planned

  2. Before the kings

    Joshua, Judges, Ruth

    The conquest, the long slide of the judges, and the Moabite widow who became David’s great-grandmother.

    Planned

  3. The united kingdom

    1–2 Samuel, 1 Kings 1–11, 1 Chronicles

    Samuel, Saul, David and Solomon: who anointed, spared, betrayed or killed whom, and the promise to David that the rest of the story leans on.

    Open

  4. The divided kingdom

    1 Kings 12 – 2 Kings 25, 2 Chronicles

    The kings of Judah and Israel side by side, the prophets sent to them, and the nations around them, down to the fall of Jerusalem.

    Open

  5. Exile and return

    Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther, Tobit, Judith

    Life in Babylon and Persia, and the rebuilding of Jerusalem and its Temple.

    Next

  6. Under the Greeks

    1–2 Maccabees

    The revolt against Antiochus, the rededication of the Temple, and the world the New Testament opens into.

    Planned

The prophets

Each prophet lives in the section where he worked, beside the kings he confronted. Samuel, Gad and Nathan are in the united kingdom; Ahijah through Ezekiel are in the divided kingdom.

Alongside the story

Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, Wisdom and Sirach sit outside the timeline, so they’ll get their own treatment once the narrative is done. Planned.

Follows the Catholic canon of forty-six Old Testament books. Dates are approximate; where scholars disagree, the notes say so.
